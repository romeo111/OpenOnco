Notas

Adult GCT is defined molecularly by the FOXL2 p.C134W mutation (present in ~97% of cases), which is the only pathognomonic molecular alteration in any solid tumor. FOXL2 is a granulosa cell transcription factor; the C134W mutation is a dominant-negative gain-of-function that prevents normal granulosa cell differentiation. Clinically, tumors present with estrogen excess (abnormal uterine bleeding, endometrial pathology) or abdominal mass. Prognosis is generally favorable for stage I (90% 10-yr OS) but late recurrences (10–20 years) are characteristic. No FDA-approved targeted therapy as of 2026; FOXL2 inhibition and CDK4/6 inhibitors are in clinical trials. FOXL2 mutation testing is diagnostic, not therapeutic selection (ESCAT IIB — clinical utility as diagnostic/prognostic marker).
