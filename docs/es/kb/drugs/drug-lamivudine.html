Datos sobre drogas

ClaseInhibidor de la transcriptasa reversa nucleosídica (INTI); inhibidor de polimerasa del VHB
MecanismoCytosine analog phosphorylated intracellularly to lamivudine triphosphate, which competes with dCTP for incorporation into HBV DNA / HIV RNA — leading to chain termination. Active against both HIV and HBV. For HBV: lamivudine has been largely supplanted by entecavir, tenofovir DF, and tenofovir AF due to high rates of resistance (~70% at 5 years). Retains role in resource-limited settings, post-transplant prophylaxis, and as part of HIV ART.
Dosificación típicaHBV: 100 mg PO once daily. HIV (as part of ART): 150 mg PO BID OR 300 mg PO once daily (always combined with other antiretrovirals). Renal adjustment: CrCl 30-49: 100 mg first dose, then 50 mg daily (HBV) CrCl 15-29: 100 mg first, then 25 mg daily CrCl 5-14: 35 mg first, then 15 mg daily CrCl <5 / dialysis: 35 mg first, then 10 mg daily

Advertencias

  • Acidosis láctica y hepatomegalia grave con esteatosis (efecto de la clase de los INTIs)
  • Exacerbaciones post-tratamiento de la hepatitis B si se interrumpe el tratamiento: monitorice estrechamente los TFT y el ADN del VHB después de interrumpir el tratamiento
  • Different formulations for HIV vs HBV — risk of HIV resistance if HBV-strength lamivudine used as HIV monotherapy in unrecognized HIV-HBV coinfection (always screen HIV before HBV monotherapy)

Notas

STUB — v0.2 prevention-workstream authoring; pending two-Clinical-Co-Lead signoff per CHARTER §6.1 dev-mode. Older HBV antiviral largely superseded by entecavir and tenofovir AF/DF due to high resistance rates (~70% at 5 years monotherapy). Retains role in: HIV ART (combined with other agents), post-liver-transplant HBV prophylaxis (combined with HBIg, then often switched to entecavir/tenofovir), resource-limited settings. NEVER use lamivudine monotherapy for HIV (rapid resistance). HBV reactivation prophylaxis: prefer entecavir or tenofovir AF over lamivudine for rituximab/anti-CD20 use.
