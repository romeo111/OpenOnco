Datos sobre drogas

ClaseTerapia de sustitución de nicotina (TRN) — acción corta bucal/sublingual
MecanismoSlowly dissolving lozenge releases nicotine across oral mucosa, producing a peak plasma level over ~20-30 minutes that blunts acute craving and withdrawal symptoms. Pharmacokinetics avoid the rapid arterial bolus that drives reinforcement from inhaled smoking. Cancer-prevention rationale: tobacco cessation reduces incidence of lung, head-and-neck, bladder, esophageal, pancreatic, cervical, kidney, AML, and other tobacco-attributable cancers (IARC Monograph 100E — Personal Habits and Indoor Combustions). NRT roughly doubles 6-month abstinence vs. placebo (Cochrane); combining a long-acting patch with a short-acting form (lozenge or gum) PRN improves quit rates over monotherapy. USPSTF Grade A for adult tobacco-cessation pharmacotherapy.
Dosificación típicaSelección de la concentración por tiempo de cigarrillo (TTFC) del día: - TTFC ≤30 min OR ≥20 cig/d: comienzo 4 mg pastilla - TTIFC >30 min O <20 ci g/d : inicio 2 mg pastina Cadencia (10-12 semanas de curso típico): - Semanas 1-6: 1 pastilla cada 1-2 h (≥9 pastillas/día al principio) - Semana 7-9: 1pastilla cada 2-4 h - Semana 10-12: 1 Pastilla cada 4-8 h Máximo 20 pastillajes/día. Permitir 20-30 min para disolverse; NO masticar ni tragar entero. Evite los alimentos/bebidas (especialmente ácidos —café, jugo, refrescos) durante 15 minutos antes/durante el uso — el pH ácido en la boca reduce drásticamente la absorción bucal.

Notas

STUB — v0.2 prevention-workstream authoring (batch 2); pending two- Clinical-Co-Lead signoff per CHARTER §6.1 dev-mode. NRT lozenge is a first-line tobacco-cessation pharmacotherapy (USPSTF Grade A for adults; insufficient evidence in pregnancy). PARTNER to DRUG-NICOTINE- PATCH (long-acting baseline) — combination patch + PRN lozenge or gum is preferred over either monotherapy (Cochrane Stead 2012). TOBACCO CESSATION is the highest-yield modifiable cancer-prevention intervention in adults — IARC class 1 for ≥17 cancer sites. Primary cessation RCTs (e.g., Shiffman 2002, West 2008) not yet ingested as standalone SRC-* entities; cited sources cover (a) the tobacco-cancer causal relationship (IARC-100A) and (b) the lung-cancer screening context where cessation is integrated (USPSTF-LUNG-LDCT-2021). Engine should surface cessation NRT options when prevention pilots flag current tobacco use; choice of patch vs lozenge vs gum vs varenicline vs bupropion belongs to the patient/clinician dialogue, not the algorithm.
