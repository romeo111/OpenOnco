Metastatic NSCLC: driver-first treatment reasoning

draft hcp_learner en

Deterministic learning chapter over OpenOnco KB entities, synthetic cases, and source records.

Learning objectives

  1. Explain why metastatic NSCLC first-line reasoning starts with actionable driver testing before PD-L1-only decisions.
  2. Distinguish EGFR, ALK, ROS1, MET exon 14, RET, BRAF V600E, and NTRK driver branches from driver-negative immunotherapy branches.
  3. Recognize PD-L1 TPS as a treatment-stratifying marker only after actionable drivers are excluded or addressed.

At a glance

  • Comprehensive molecular testing is the central first-line gate in metastatic NSCLC.
  • Actionable drivers route to targeted therapy branches before PD-L1 immunotherapy branches.
  • PD-L1 TPS still matters, but mainly in the driver-negative pathway.
  • Brain metastases, frailty, organ dysfunction, and urgent complications remain parallel MDT concerns.
  • The chapter is OpenOnco-authored educational content, not official ESMO or CME material.

Driver-first cascade

The metastatic NSCLC chapter emphasizes driver-first routing. EGFR, ALK, ROS1, MET exon 14, RET, BRAF V600E, and NTRK are evaluated before the driver-negative PD-L1 immunotherapy pathway.

EGFR branch

Classical sensitizing EGFR alterations route to the EGFR first-line branch in the OpenOnco model. PD-L1 expression should not be used to skip driver-directed reasoning.

Driver-negative pathway

When no actionable driver is found, PD-L1 TPS helps separate pembrolizumab monotherapy from chemo-immunotherapy strategies. Frailty, organ dysfunction, and urgent thoracic complications remain safety gates.

Practice questions

Question 1 type_a intro

In metastatic NSCLC, what is the first major routing concept in the OpenOnco handbook chapter?

  1. A. Actionable driver testing before PD-L1-only immunotherapy decisions
  2. B. PD-L1 monotherapy for every patient
  3. C. No molecular testing before first-line treatment
  4. D. Use chemotherapy only if EGFR testing is positive
Answer: A

The NSCLC chapter uses a driver-first cascade. Actionable drivers are evaluated before the driver-negative PD-L1 pathway.

Question 2 type_a intermediate

A metastatic NSCLC case has an EGFR exon 19 deletion. In this learning model, which branch should be considered before PD-L1 monotherapy?

  1. A. EGFR-targeted first-line branch
  2. B. Driver-negative PD-L1 high branch
  3. C. Observation only
  4. D. CRC MSI-H branch
Answer: A

Classical sensitizing EGFR alterations route to an EGFR-targeted first-line branch. PD-L1 expression should not be used to bypass driver-directed reasoning.

Question 3 type_k intermediate

Which statements are true for the OpenOnco metastatic NSCLC first-line chapter?

  1. A. PD-L1 TPS is most useful after actionable drivers are excluded or addressed.
  2. B. EGFR and ALK branches are part of the driver-first cascade.
  3. C. Brain metastases and frailty are parallel MDT/safety issues.
  4. D. Comprehensive molecular testing is irrelevant in metastatic NSCLC.
Answer: A,B,C

The chapter keeps molecular driver testing central, uses PD-L1 for the driver-negative pathway, and still surfaces safety and MDT issues such as brain metastases and frailty.
