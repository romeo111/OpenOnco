Lind and Advocate expand cancer-trial matching: agreement is not enrollment

A retrospective study found 94.3% agreement on eligibility criteria. The new partnership expands screening work; effects on trial enrollment remain to be shown.

by OpenOnco

Written retrospectively and published on November 21, 2026.

A company announcement is not independent evidence of clinical benefit. This comparison is informational, not medical advice. This article does not replace a doctor.

On 20 November 2026, Lind and Advocate Health announced an expanded partnership focused partly on cancer clinical trials. Lind says its institution-facing platform screens electronic health records against trial criteria, shows source-linked evidence, and leaves enrollment decisions to clinicians and research teams. The expansion covers study feasibility, patient identification, screening, enrollment coordination and portfolio analysis; it is a plan for wider work, not a reported rise in trial enrollment.

A June 2026 JCO Oncology Practice study coauthored by Lind and health-system researchers reported 94.3% agreement with expert reviewers at the eligibility-criterion level in a retrospective cancer-data evaluation. That measures matching against reviewed criteria, not whether more people entered trials or benefited from treatment. The study used one health system; incomplete records and complex multi-arm trials limited generalization. Lind’s terms require authorized institutional users and warn that a suggested match does not establish eligibility or an available place. The public site offers a demo, with no public per-user price or source-code licence stated.

OpenOnco is free to read, with MIT-licensed code, inspectable rules and cited oncology knowledge. Its structured case tools make discussion drafts; they do not screen hospital records against live trial protocols or coordinate enrollment. Clinical review of OpenOnco content remains pending. Lind’s patient-to-trial workflow and OpenOnco’s public knowledge workflow could complement each other; there is no direct accuracy or patient-outcome comparison. Sources checked 21 November 2026. This article does not replace a doctor.
