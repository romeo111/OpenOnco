This section is new, and it is open for comment

Release notes and governance decisions now have a home on the site instead of living only in commit messages. Every post takes comments — with a few rules that a cancer-information site genuinely needs.

by OpenOnco

Until now, everything about how OpenOnco changes lived in commit messages, review documents and specs. That is fine for contributors reading the repository and useless for a clinician who wants to know what moved this month. This section is the fix.

Posts are written in both English and Ukrainian, and they are checked into the repository as structured YAML under content/news/. They are built by the same pipeline as the rest of the site, so a post is reviewable as a diff like everything else here.

Why comments have rules

Open comments on a site about cancer treatment carry two risks that a comment box on a normal software project does not, so it is worth being direct about both.

The first is that people post their own medical details. A comment thread is public, permanent and indexed by search engines. Please do not put a diagnosis, test results, dates, or anything identifying you or a patient into a comment — a public page is the wrong place for it, and once it is indexed it is not really retractable.

The second is that comments can read as advice. They are not. Comments are readers' opinions; they are not reviewed by the clinical leads, and they carry none of the source-citation guarantees that the knowledge base does. Nothing on this site replaces your treating oncologist.

Nobody monitors comments for urgent problems. In a clinical emergency, contact your treating team or emergency services — not a web page.

Where to report a clinical error

If you have found something clinically wrong in the knowledge base, a comment is not the fastest route. Open an issue tagged clinical-error instead: per CHARTER §9.1 that path has a triage SLA of 48 hours and an assessment SLA of 7 days, and it produces a public record of the fix.

How comments work technically

Comments are hosted on GitHub Discussions through giscus, so the project stores no commenter data of its own and there is no third-party advertising or tracking involved.

The widget is not loaded until you press the button. Simply reading a post sends no request to giscus or GitHub — which matters more than usual when the page someone is quietly reading is about their diagnosis.

  • Nothing here is monitored for urgent problems. For a clinical emergency, contact your treating team or emergency services.
  • For a clinical error in the knowledge base, open a clinical-error issue instead — that path has a triage SLA.
