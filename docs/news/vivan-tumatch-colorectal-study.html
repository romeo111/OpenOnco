Vivan plans a TuMatch study in colorectal cancer: the evidence is still to come

A prospective study in Abu Dhabi is set to include at least 10 people. Its start is a research milestone, not proof that treatment recommendations improve outcomes.

by OpenOnco

Written retrospectively and published on November 22, 2026.

A company announcement is not independent evidence of clinical benefit. This comparison is informational, not medical advice. This article does not replace a doctor.

On 12 November 2026, Vivan Therapeutics and Hub71 said a prospective study of TuMatch was cleared to begin at Burjeel Cancer Institute in Abu Dhabi. The company says the colorectal-cancer study will enrol at least 10 people and collect clinical and health-economics data. These are planned measurements, not published outcomes. The announcement gives no trial registry identifier or protocol. Permission to conduct a study is not clearance for routine clinical use; Vivan says the future data will support its CE-mark and FDA submissions.

TuMatch uses a tumour genomic profile, Vivan's proprietary drug-response dataset from fruit-fly models, and published evidence to rank options for an oncologist. The public site describes a focus on colorectal, lung, pancreatic and other gastrointestinal cancers; it does not publish a user price or a source-code licence. A 2026 paper on personalised fruit-fly models examined three colorectal-cancer model lines and found improved fly survival in two under experimental treatment. That is laboratory evidence, not proof of patient benefit or clinical validation of TuMatch. The announcement does not report treatment-response or survival results from the Abu Dhabi study.

OpenOnco offers free, cited oncology knowledge, inspectable rules and MIT-licensed code. Its structured case tools prepare drafts for discussion; they do not run fly-model experiments or predict individual drug response from a tumour genome. Vivan's experimental matching workflow and OpenOnco's public evidence workflow address different tasks. No direct comparison of accuracy or patient outcomes is available.
