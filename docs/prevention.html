Cancer prevention

Most cancers do not start the day they are diagnosed — they accumulate over years of risk that can be measured, modified, or screened away. OpenOnco's prevention surface gathers the entities most relevant to risk reduction and early detection: risk factors, screening protocols, vaccines, hereditary syndromes, and lifestyle modifications. They live inside the same Onco Wiki as the rest of the knowledge base, with the same source-grounded provenance.

Risk factors

Modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors per cancer type — tobacco, alcohol, obesity, occupational exposures, viral infections, family history.

Browse Onco Wiki → Screening protocols

Population and high-risk screening for breast, colorectal, cervical, lung, and prostate cancers — eligibility, modality, intervals, evidence.

Browse Onco Wiki → Vaccines

HPV and HBV vaccination as primary prevention for cervical, oropharyngeal, anal, and hepatocellular cancers — schedules and indications.

Browse Onco Wiki → Hereditary syndromes

Lynch, BRCA1/2, Li-Fraumeni, FAP and other syndromes — testing criteria, surveillance, and risk-reducing options.

Browse Onco Wiki → Lifestyle modifications

Evidence-graded interventions: smoking cessation, alcohol reduction, weight management, physical activity, diet patterns.

Browse Onco Wiki → By disease

Each disease page lists the prevention entities (risk factors, screening, vaccines) attached to it — start from a specific cancer type.

What this surface is

An entry point, not a separate KB

Prevention entities live in the same Onco Wiki as everything else. This page is a curated entry for people thinking about risk and screening, not a second knowledge base.

Source-grounded, like the rest

Every claim has a Source citation — guidelines, peer-reviewed evidence, or regulatory documents. No invented statements; no "AI consensus" summaries.

Population and individual

Where guidance differs for general population vs. high-risk groups (family history, prior exposure, syndromic), entries make the distinction explicit.

Not a substitute for a clinician

A risk assessment is not a diagnosis. For individual decisions about screening, testing, or vaccination, work with a qualified physician.
