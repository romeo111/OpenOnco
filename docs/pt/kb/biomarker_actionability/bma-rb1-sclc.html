Resumo das provasRB1 biallelic loss, together with TP53 loss, is the molecular hallmark of SCLC (~95% of cases have both). RB1 loss is not used to select therapy in SCLC (all SCLC patients receive the same backbone: EP/EC ± atezolizumab/durvalumab); rather, it is a diagnostic characteristic and potential therapeutic vulnerability. RB1 loss renders cells dependent on alternative cell cycle regulators (CDK4/6-independent E2F activation) — this is why CDK4/6 inhibitors have no activity in SCLC (pRb loss negates CDK4/6 inhibition). Investigational targeting of RB1-deficient SCLC: aurora kinase A inhibitors (alisertib — phase II, limited activity); EZH2 inhibitors (tazemetostat — RB1-null tumors may have heightened EZH2 dependence); LSD1 inhibitors; ATR/CHK1 inhibitors (replication stress in RB1-null context). ESCAT IIIB: RB1 loss is diagnostically informative and a conceptual therapeutic target, but no FDA-approved targeted therapy exploiting RB1 deficiency as of 2026.

Notas

ESCAT IIIB: A perda de RB1 no SCLC é um marcador de resistência e característica diagnóstica (CDK4/6i), mas não seleciona a terapia direcionada ativa. A perda da proteína Rb pelo IHC é usada clinicamente para distinguir SCLC de outras neoplasias neuroendocrinas de alto grau: Rb-negativo (SCLC) vs Rb retenido (tumor carcinoide, LCNEC pode ser variável). Os subtipos moleculares SCLC (ASCL1-alta, NEUROD1- alta, POU2F3-alta e YAP1- elevada) estão emergentes como preditores de DLL3 (rovalpituzumab tesirina/tarlatamab) e resposta inibidora do LSD1, mas são biomarcadores separados do RB1. Tarlatamabe (DLL3-alvo biTE; FDA 2024 para 2L+ SCLC) não seleciona com base no estado RB1 .
